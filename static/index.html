02 THE MARKET MAP

Different instruments.
Different jobs.

A share is not a fund. A bond is not a savings account. A crypto token is not a business. Learn what you own before looking at a price.

Shares & stocks

A share represents part ownership in a company. Its price can reflect expectations, results, rates, and sentiment. Learn to read annual reports, cash flow, debt, and dilution—not just a ticker chart.

Funds & ETFs

A fund pools investments; an ETF trades on an exchange. Broad index funds can spread company-specific risk, but still fall with the market. Check holdings, fees, currency, tracking, and the index methodology.

Bonds & gilts

A bond is a loan to a government or company. You may receive interest and repayment, but issuer default, inflation, and interest-rate changes matter. When market rates rise, existing fixed-rate bond prices often fall.

03 ACCOUNTS & TAX WRAPPERS

Where you hold it
can matter, too.

An account wrapper can affect tax and access. It does not make the thing inside it risk-free.

UK QUICK GUIDE · TAX YEAR 2026–27

ISAs, in plain English.

An Individual Savings Account can shelter eligible savings or investments from UK tax on interest, income, and gains. The annual overall ISA allowance is £20,000 for 2026–27, across your ISAs.

  • Cash ISA: savings interest in a tax-free wrapper.
  • Stocks and Shares ISA: eligible investments such as shares, funds, and bonds; values can fall.
  • Lifetime ISA: specific first-home or later-life rules, with a withdrawal charge in many other cases.
  • Innovative Finance ISA: certain peer-to-peer investments; lending can default.

From 6 April 2027, the cash ISA limit is scheduled to be £12,000 for under-65s within the same £20,000 overall allowance. Rules can change: check GOV.UK before acting.

A USEFUL DISTINCTION

“Bond” can mean two things.

A government or corporate bond is a tradable loan. Its price moves; the borrower can fail to repay.

An investment bond is often an insurance-company product invested in funds, with its own lock-ins, charges, and tax treatment. It is not the same as a government gilt.

Before buying either, ask: who owes the money, when can I get out, what could I lose, what are the fees, and which tax rules apply?

04 A BEGINNER’S ROUTE IN

Build the habit
before the stake.

Whether you mean earning through a finance career or learning to invest, useful beginnings are built on skills and repeatable practice.

WEEK 01

Learn the vocabulary

Market orders, limit orders, bid/ask spread, liquidity, market cap, yield, duration, diversification, custody, and fees.

WEEK 02

Read primary sources

Company filings, fund factsheets, bond terms, and crypto protocol documentation. Separate a source’s claims from independent evidence.

WEEK 03

Keep a paper journal

Record the thesis, evidence, risks, time horizon, and what would disprove it. Track hypothetical results after fees and taxes.

WEEK 04

Review, don’t chase

Look for mistakes and missing assumptions. Avoid leverage, urgency, and putting essential money into experiments.
